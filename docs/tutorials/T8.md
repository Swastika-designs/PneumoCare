# T8 — Healthcare Strategic Planning Part 1

**Where in the app:** Operations Analytics → **KPIs & comparisons** tab (`/operations?tab=kpis`)  
**Status:** verified - 4/4 workflow checks passed against the live API (2026-10-28, `scripts/verify_tutorials.py`)

## Objective

Compute healthcare key performance indicators (KPIs), compare groups statistically and follow trends.

## Key concepts

- **KPIs.** Encounters, unique patients, encounters per patient, total and average claim cost, payer coverage %, uninsured share, inpatient admissions, average length of stay (LOS), 30-day readmission rate, ED visits per 1,000 patients.
- **30-day readmission.** An inpatient admission followed by another admission of the same patient within 30 days of discharge.
- **Group comparison.** Costs are strongly right-skewed, so a rank-based **Kruskal–Wallis H** test (or Mann–Whitney U for 2 groups) is used instead of ANOVA.
- **Dashboards.** KPI cards, group bar charts, a monthly trend line and yearly unique patients.

## Dataset

Synthea encounters joined with patients (gender, age group at encounter), payers and organizations.

## Methodology

Filter by start year → compute headline KPIs → group by encounter class, year, gender, age group, payer or organisation → run the rank test on the chosen metric (claim cost, base cost or duration) for groups with ≥ 5 encounters → monthly and yearly aggregates.

## Implementation

- Code: `backend/app/services/operations.py` (`kpis`, `_readmission_rate`), `frontend/src/pages/Operations.tsx` (`KpiTab`)
- API: `GET /api/operations/kpis?group_by=&metric=&start_year=`

## Outputs

KPI cards, encounters and average cost by group, test result with interpretation, group table, trends.

## Results (actual run, 26 Oct 2026)

2016–2026: 3,935 encounters, 101 patients, average claim cost $3,301, payer coverage 71.1%, 4.9% uninsured encounters, 39 admissions, average LOS 5.0 days, 30-day readmission 0%. Cost differs strongly by encounter class (Kruskal–Wallis H = 307.8, p = 8.9 × 10⁻⁶²); SNF and inpatient stays are the most expensive.
