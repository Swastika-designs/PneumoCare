# T14 — Behavioral Factors Influencing Adult Weight Part 1

**Where in the app:** Advanced Analytics → **Adult weight: exploration** tab (`/advanced?tab=weight-eda`)  
**Status:** verified - 3/3 workflow checks passed against the live API (2026-10-28, `scripts/verify_tutorials.py`)

## Objective

Describe adult BMI and lifestyle factors and how they relate.

## Key concepts

- **Recoding survey data.** NHANES uses codes such as 7/9/77/99/777/7777/9999 for refused or don't know, and these become missing. Activity minutes = days × minutes for work, transport and recreation; vigorous minutes count double (moderate-equivalent minutes per week). Alcohol frequency codes are converted to drinking days per year, then multiplied by drinks per day. Smoking: never / former / current. Diet values come from day-1 recall and are kept only if the recall was reliable.
- **BMI categories (WHO).** < 18.5 underweight, 18.5–25 normal, 25–30 overweight, ≥ 30 obese.
- **Spearman correlation.** Rank correlation, robust to skewed variables such as alcohol and activity.
- **Survey weights.** NHANES over-samples some groups. The MEC exam weight `WTMEC2YR` gives a population estimate (a point estimate only here).
- **Group comparisons.** Mean BMI and obesity % by activity level, sleep band, smoking, education, sex and race/ethnicity, each with a Kruskal–Wallis test.

## Dataset

NHANES 2017–2018: DEMO_J, BMX_J, PAQ_J, SLQ_J, SMQ_J, ALQ_J and DR1TOT_J merged on SEQN; adults ≥ 20 years, not pregnant, BMI measured. **n = 5,120**.

## Methodology

Merge → filter → recode → descriptive table → histogram (values above the 99th percentile grouped) → BMI categories → weighted and unweighted obesity prevalence → Spearman matrix → group tables with tests.

## Outputs

Stat cards, distribution and BMI-category charts, mean BMI by group with a test, correlation table and heat-map, descriptive statistics, summary by sex.

## Results (actual run, 26 Oct 2026)

Obesity prevalence is 41.8% unweighted and **42.5% survey-weighted**, consistent with the CDC's published estimate of about 42% for 2017–2018. That is a useful validation of the recoding. The strongest BMI correlate is sedentary minutes per day (ρ = +0.10); alcohol (−0.09), fibre (−0.07) and sleep (−0.06) are weaker. Mean BMI is 30.3 for inactive adults and 29.6 for those meeting the activity guideline (Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.008).

## Limitations

Cross-sectional data, so no causal claims. Activity, sleep and alcohol are self-reported. Diet comes from a single 24-hour recall. Weighted estimates have no design-based standard errors.
